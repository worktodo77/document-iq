## 0. Headline, stated the way D-25 asked for it

**Criterion 4 is NOT met, and the targeted footer re-OCR is not what will close
it.** The pass does what D-25 ruled — it reads the stamp band as its own
recognition problem and gets a reading where the whole-page pass got none — and
the measurement then runs into a wall that has nothing to do with resolution:

> **rapidocr reads this production's stamp DIGITS correctly and cannot resolve
> its PREFIX.** On the pages the baseline missed, the band pass returns
> `iCON004926`, `jiCON004926`, `liCON002291` and `TiCON005000` for a stamp that
> reads `iiCON004926`. **The six digits are right essentially every time. The
> doubled lower-case `ii` is wrong essentially every time.**

More resolution makes it worse, not better (600 dpi and 800 dpi both score below
400). Widening the detector's box does not touch it. Binarizing the band helps a
little and truncates digits on other pages. This is the recognition model, and
it is the first hard, page-level evidence for the thing D-19 recorded as an
unquantified risk: *"the tool ships an OCR engine chosen on in-house familiarity
and ONNX bundling convenience, never benchmarked against an alternative on this
corpus."*

Per D-25's own terms — *"if it does not [reach 99%], D-19 is back on the table on
evidence"* — this is that evidence.

**The failure direction is preserved throughout: 0 wrong, 0 false positives.**
Every shortfall is still an absent locator. A mangled prefix does not match the
operator-confirmed format, so the page stays a flagged miss rather than acquiring
a locator that is not in the production.

| | baseline (`build/sprint-2`) | after D-25 |
|---|---|---|
| overall | **593 / 648 = 91.512%** | *§3* |

### 1.1 The mechanism

A page qualifies when **both** hold:

1. DocIQ had to OCR it — it had no usable text layer. A native-text page never
   pays anything, and on the acceptance sample that is 568 of 648 pages.
2. Its ordinary reading yielded **no stamp-shaped line anywhere in the Bates
   zone** (`bates.zone_has_candidate`, the same grammar detection uses).

For such a page the bottom **1.25 inches** of the page is re-rendered at **400
dpi**, tiled, and recognized on its own. Only the stamp-shaped tokens of that
reading are merged back, appended to the tail of the page text where the zone
will see them. The top band is read too — §4 says "corners/footers" and a
header-stamped production is ordinary — but only on the pages the bottom band
did not resolve.

The trigger is format-agnostic on purpose: extraction is Stage 1 and the
production's format is not confirmed until Stage 3, so the only question that
can be asked is "did the ordinary pass produce anything stamp-shaped at all?".

### 1.2 It appends TEXT, never a locator

Nothing in the pass writes `PageRecord.bates`. It contributes candidate text,
which Stage 3 judges with the same grammar and the same operator-confirmed
format as any other text. That is the whole reason a misread band cannot become
a wrong locator: `jiCON004926` is not the confirmed format, so it is ignored and
the page stays a miss.

## 2. Defects found and fixed

### 2.1 The appended block could evict the page's own reading (found by a test)

Appending to the tail is what makes a recovered stamp reachable, and it is also
what pushes the existing tail out of the zone. That would have turned pages
`apply_bates` currently gets right — via the confirmed-token search — into
misses.

Fixed by construction, not by luck: `BatesZone.tail_lines` is
`_TAIL_LINES_BASE + FOOTER_BLOCK_MAX_LINES` (4 + 4 = 8), and a test asserts the
relation so raising one without the other fails. A second, parametrized test
walks **every** combination of original-tail length × appended-block length up
to both bounds and asserts nothing falls out of the zone.

**Fail-before, watched:** with the margin removed, **10 of the 16** parametrized
cases go red.

### 2.2 An anchored line outranked a folded one — which turns RIGHT into WRONG

This is the serious one, and it did not exist until D-25 created it.

`apply_bates` used to run the whole-line anchored match first and fall back to
the confirmed-token search only for pages with no anchored line. The band pass
appends a recovered token **as its own line**, which is anchored. So a band pass
that misread one digit would produce an anchored `iiCON003945` that beat the
folded, correct `iiCON003944` already in the page's zone — and a page that was
right would have become **wrong**, which is the one outcome §4 rates worse than
a miss.

There is one rule now (`_zone_stamp`): every confirmed-format match anywhere in
the zone is collected, and two different ones are a **refusal**, wherever they
came from. The anchored case is a special case of it, not a privileged path.

**Fail-before, watched:** restoring anchored-first turns the disagreement test
red, and it fails by producing `iiCON003945` — the wrong locator, exactly.

### 2.3 The band was a fraction of page height, which is wrong twice over

Found by measuring, not by reading. The acceptance corpus contains pages whose
box is **2700 × 3681 points** — photographs 37 by 51 inches — and 3600 × 2761
landscape ones.

* **Cost.** 14% of a 51-inch page is a seven-inch strip: 15000 × 2863 px at 400
  dpi, **43 Mpx per band**. A sweep over 55 such pages **did not finish in 90
  minutes** and had to be killed. Mean band was 35 Mpx at 400 dpi and 79 Mpx at
  600.
* **Correctness.** At 3600pt wide the strip is **13:1**, past rapidocr's
  `width_height_ratio` of 8. The engine then **skips text detection entirely**
  and recognizes the whole strip as a single line. Measured directly: the bottom
  strip of `iiCON004938.pdf` p1 and `iiCON006088.pdf` p1 returned **nothing
  readable at all** — on precisely the pages the pass exists for.

A Bates stamp is burned in at a physical size, a physical distance from the
physical edge. Measured on this corpus, it sits within **~26–40 points of the
bottom edge** whether the page is letter-size or four feet tall. So the band is
**1.25 inches, always**, and it is **tiled** at the engine's own aspect limit
with a fifth of a tile of overlap so a stamp cannot be cut in half.

A tile is at most `band × 8·band` — **~1.7 Mpx at 400 dpi whatever the page
is** — and the tile *count* grows with page width alone. The 43 Mpx band became
5 tiles totalling ~10 Mpx, against the 77 Mpx the ordinary whole-page pass
already rasterizes for the same page. Nine page shapes are asserted, including
the three real ones from the corpus, plus a page smaller than the band is deep.

### 2.4 A latent hazard, recorded rather than triggered

`RapidOCR.__call__` mutates the **shared** detector's `box_thresh` and
`unclip_ratio` when it is passed per-call keyword arguments, and does **not**
restore them. A single call with tuning parameters would silently re-tune every
later call in the process, making a run depend on document order. DocIQ never
passes them — this was found while testing whether detector tuning fixed the
prefix (§4.3) — so it is a hazard, not a live defect, and it is recorded here
because the fix for it is "never pass them", which is invisible in the code.

## 4. What the band pass cannot fix, with the evidence

### 4.1 The prefix

Over the pages the baseline missed, with the tiled 1.25-inch band:

| setting | stamp recovered exactly | what it returned otherwise |
|---|---|---|
| 300 dpi | 1 / 12 | `iCON…`, `jiCON…` |
| **400 dpi** | **1 / 12** | `iCON…`, `jiCON…` |
| 600 dpi | 0 / 12 | `jiCON…`, and one truncation `CON005` |
| 400 dpi, 2-inch band | 0 / 12 | `iCON…`, `jiCON…` |

The digits are correct in **every** one of those readings. Only the prefix is
wrong, and it is wrong in the same way every time: the doubled lower-case `ii`
resolves to one glyph, or to `j`, `l` or `T`.

### 4.2 Resolution is not the variable

| variant | exact | note |
|---|---|---|
| 400 dpi raw | 1 / 10 | |
| 400 dpi + Otsu binarization | 3 / 10 | but truncated digits on one page (`jiCON005`) |
| 400 dpi grayscale | 1 / 10 | |
| 250 dpi raw | 0 / 10 | |
| 250 dpi + Otsu | 1 / 10 | |
| 800 dpi raw | 0 / 10 | five pages returned nothing at all |

Otsu binarization is **measured and not adopted**: on ten pages it is better on
the prefix and it introduced a digit truncation, and neither effect is large
enough to change the verdict. Adopting an image transform that can shorten a
digit run, on that evidence, would be tuning towards a number rather than
towards a correct locator.

### 4.3 Detector tuning is not the variable either

The obvious hypothesis was that the detection box starts a glyph too far right
and clips the first `i`. Tested directly by passing `unclip_ratio` 2.2 and 3.0
and `box_thresh` 0.3 to the engine:

| variant | exact |
|---|---|
| default (1.6 / 0.5) | 1 / 10 |
| unclip 2.2 | 0 / 10 |
| unclip 3.0 | 0 / 10 |
| unclip 2.2, box_thresh 0.3 | 0 / 10 |

Widening the box does not recover the glyph. It is not a cropping problem.

### 4.4 What WOULD close it, and why it is a ruling and not a commit

The prefix carries **no per-page information** — every page of a confirmed
production has the same one. Normalizing a near-miss prefix to the confirmed one
(digits, digit width, separator and suffix all matching exactly) would convert
most of this residue into correct locators, and could not point at a *different
page*.

It is not done here, for a reason that is not timidity:

* §4 requires that misses are **flagged, not silently corrected**. Writing
  `iiCON004926` where the page reads `jiCON004926` is a silent correction.
* `bates.py` states, as a deliberate rule, that `iiCON` and `IICON` are two
  formats and neither is applied to the other's pages, *"because a locator
  pointing at a record that does not exist is worse than no locator."*
  Prefix normalization softens that rule for OCR'd pages.
* On a corpus holding both an `iiCON` and a real `iCON` production, it would
  misattribute — narrow, but real.

**This is a decision for Alex, with numbers attached**, and it is exactly the
kind of trade D-25 reserved. The alternative on the table is D-19.
